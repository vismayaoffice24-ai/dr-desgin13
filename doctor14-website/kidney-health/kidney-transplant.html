Definitive Renal Therapy

The Journey to Kidney Transplantation

For eligible patients experiencing end-stage renal failure, kidney transplantation represents the gold standard therapy—restoring natural filtration, liberating you from dialysis machines, and greatly enhancing longevity.

Why Transplant is the Preferred Treatment

While dialysis cleans blood intermittently for a few hours per week, a successfully transplanted kidney functions continuously 24 hours a day. It restores natural hormone regulation (normalizing red blood cell counts and bone metabolism), frees patients from rigid fluid restrictions, and offers a significantly higher life expectancy compared to long-term dialysis.

Preemptive Kidney Transplantation

The ideal time to undergo a kidney transplant is before ever starting dialysis (known as a preemptive transplant). Planning early when eGFR approaches 15–20 mL/min allows time for thorough donor matching and avoids dialysis-associated vascular complications.

Sources of Donor Kidneys

Kidney transplants originate from two primary pathways:

  • Living Related or Compatible Donor: A healthy family member or emotionally related donor willingly donates one kidney. Extensive medical and psychiatric evaluations ensure the donor has two healthy kidneys and will suffer no long-term health deficit. Living donor kidneys function immediately and typically last 15 to 25+ years.
  • Deceased (Cadaveric) Donor: Kidneys donated by individuals who have suffered irreversible brain death and whose families consent to organ donation. Patients are registered on state and national allocation waiting registries.

The Pre-Transplant Evaluation Process

To ensure safety and optimal graft survival, both recipient and prospective donor undergo comprehensive clinical profiling:

  • ABO Blood Group Matching: Ensuring primary blood group compatibility (or preparing specialized desensitization protocols for ABO-incompatible transplants).
  • HLA Tissue Typing: Assessing Human Leukocyte Antigen matching between recipient and donor to determine immunologic similarity.
  • Complement-Dependent Cytotoxicity (CDC) & Flow Cross-Match: Mixing recipient blood serum with donor cells to confirm no pre-existing antibodies exist that could cause hyperacute graft rejection.
  • Cardiopulmonary & Infection Screening: Coronary angiography, echocardiography, dental clearance, and viral screenings (Hepatitis, CMV, EBV, TB) to ensure safety under future immunosuppression.

The Surgical Procedure & The New Kidney

Many patients are surprised to learn that their original failing kidneys are almost never removed unless they are chronically infected, causing uncontrollable high blood pressure, or severely enlarged by polycystic disease.

  • The new donor kidney is placed in the lower abdomen (iliac fossa) through a curved lower pelvic incision.
  • The donor renal artery and vein are connected to the recipient's external iliac blood vessels.
  • The donor ureter is connected directly to your urinary bladder.
  • The surgery takes approximately 3 to 4 hours, and most patients are walking within 24 to 48 hours.

Lifelong Immunosuppressive Care

Because the transplanted kidney carries foreign cell markers, your immune system would naturally try to reject it. To protect the graft, patients take lifelong antirejection medications:

  • Calcineurin Inhibitors (Tacrolimus or Cyclosporine): The cornerstone of immunosuppression, titrated carefully based on precise 12-hour trough blood levels.
  • Antiproliferative Agents (Mycophenolate Mofetil): Prevents rapid multiplication of attacking immune lymphocytes.
  • Corticosteroids (Prednisolone): Used at low baseline maintenance doses to suppress inflammation.
  • Strict Medication Adherence: Missing even a single dose can trigger acute cellular or antibody-mediated rejection. Routine blood checks allow Dr. Shyma Sunil to maintain exact therapeutic drug levels.
