Kidney Stones: Symptoms & Prevention

Kidney stones (nephrolithiasis) cause some of the most excruciating pain known in medicine. While acute removal is handled surgically, preventing future recurrences requires a medical nephrology evaluation.

Why Do Kidney Stones Form?

Kidney stones form when mineral salts and chemicals in your urine become supersaturated—precipitating out of solution to form microscopic crystals that gradually aggregate into solid mineral stones.

More than 50% of people who form a single kidney stone will develop another within 5 to 7 years unless the underlying biochemical trigger is diagnosed and treated. Recurrent stone disease can lead to chronic hydronephrosis, recurrent urinary infections, and permanent kidney scarring.

Red Flag Emergency: Stone with Fever

If kidney stone pain is accompanied by shivering, high fever, or an inability to pass urine, this indicates an infected, obstructed kidney (obstructive pyelonephritis). This is a life-threatening medical emergency requiring immediate hospital admission to decompress the urinary tract.

The Four Major Stone Types

Treating and preventing stones depends entirely on mineral composition:

  • Calcium Oxalate Stones (75–80%): The most common stone type, often precipitated by dehydration, high dietary sodium, excessive oxalate foods (spinach, chocolate, black tea), or low urinary citrate.
  • Uric Acid Stones (5–10%): Occur when urine is abnormally acidic (pH < 5.5), common in patients with high animal purine intake, obesity, metabolic syndrome, or gout. These stones can often be medically dissolved by urine alkalinization.
  • Struvite / Infection Stones (10%): Composed of magnesium ammonium phosphate, triggered by chronic urinary infections with urease-producing bacteria (like Proteus). These can grow into massive "staghorn" stones filling the renal pelvis.
  • Cystine Stones (1%): A rare genetic metabolic disorder (cystinuria) causing kidneys to excrete excessive amounts of the amino acid cystine.

The Role of Nephrology: 24-Hour Metabolic Workup

While urologists break or extract obstructing stones, a nephrologist determines why your body is manufacturing stones. For recurrent or high-risk stone formers, Dr. Shyma Sunil conducts a detailed metabolic investigation:

  • 24-Hour Urine Collection: Analyzes total volume, urinary calcium, oxalate, citrate, uric acid, sodium, phosphorus, and urinary pH.
  • Serum Parathyroid Hormone (PTH) & Calcium: Screening for underlying primary hyperparathyroidism or hypercalcemia.
  • Stone Crystallography: Chemical infrared spectroscopy of any passed or surgically extracted stone fragments.

Evidence-Based Prevention Strategies

Following tailored medical guidance reduces stone recurrence rates by over 80%:

  • Target 2.5 Liters of Urine Output: Drink sufficient clean water throughout the day and before bedtime so that your urine remains consistently pale.
  • Do NOT Avoid Dietary Calcium: Restricting calcium actually increases stone risk, because dietary calcium binds oxalate in your gut. Consume normal amounts of dietary dairy while restricting excess sodium and animal purines.
  • Boost Urinary Citrate: Citrate is nature's most potent crystal inhibitor. Fresh lemon water and prescribed potassium citrate syrups prevent calcium oxalate aggregation.
  • Restrict Salt: High sodium intake forces the kidneys to excrete more calcium into the urine (hypercalciuria), fueling crystal formation.
