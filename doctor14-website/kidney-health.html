Patient Education & Staging Guide

Understanding Kidney Health

Your kidneys are quiet, tirelessly working filters. Because kidney damage often develops without pain or obvious symptoms, understanding your filtration rate (eGFR) and clinical stages is your strongest defense against disease progression.

Clinical Staging Tool

CKD Stages by eGFR

Estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate (eGFR) measures how many milliliters of blood your kidneys clean per minute, adjusted for body surface area (mL/min/1.73m²). Staging guides clinical monitoring and medication selection.

Stage 1 · Normal or High Kidney Function eGFR ≥ 90 mL/min

Kidney filtration rate is normal, but structural or laboratory signs of kidney damage exist (such as protein/albumin in urine, abnormal ultrasound findings, or polycystic disease). Focus is on strict blood sugar and BP control to prevent functional decline.

Stage 2 · Mild Reduction in Function eGFR 60 – 89 mL/min

Subtle decrease in filtration capacity alongside persistent urine protein or anatomical changes. Often asymptomatic; requires annual nephrology checks, lipid management, and careful avoidance of nephrotoxic medications like NSAID painkillers.

Stage 3 (3a & 3b) · Moderate Reduction in Function eGFR 30 – 59 mL/min

Kidneys are operating at approximately half their full capacity. Waste products like blood urea and serum creatinine begin rising. Patients may experience mild fatigue, nocturnal urination, or mild fluid retention. Regular nephrology management and dietary adjustments are vital.

Stage 4 · Severe Reduction in Function eGFR 15 – 29 mL/min

Advanced kidney damage with prominent clinical signs such as swelling of ankles, anemia, metabolic bone disease, and electrolyte shifts. Proactive planning for future renal replacement therapy (AV fistula creation or transplant evaluation) is established here.

Stage 5 · Kidney Failure (End-Stage Renal Disease) eGFR < 15 mL/min

The kidneys can no longer keep up with basic fluid and waste excretion. Symptoms include nausea, loss of appetite, breathlessness, and severe edema. Active renal replacement therapy (maintenance hemodialysis, peritoneal dialysis) or kidney transplantation is required.
